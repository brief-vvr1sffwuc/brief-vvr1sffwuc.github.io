Why do these dates matter?

A company's results day is when its share price usually moves most, up or down, and suppliers often move with their big customers. Results dates are estimates until the company confirms them, and they can change. Knowing a date is coming is a reason to expect bigger swings, not a reason to buy or sell.

Words explained

Alert
A note that something changed at a company you follow. It's information, not advice.
Market mood
A score from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed). It combines how nervous option traders are (the VIX) with how far big tech stocks have run above their usual level.
VIX
The "fear index". It rises when investors expect big swings in the stock market.
Chip stocks (SMH)
A fund holding the biggest chip companies, such as Nvidia and TSMC. It shows how the chip industry did as a whole.
Big tech stocks (QQQ)
A fund holding the 100 biggest companies on the Nasdaq exchange.
Supplier
A company that sells to another. When Microsoft spends more on data centres, its suppliers (chips, servers, power, cooling) get the orders.
Context only
Shown to help you understand the day. It was tested and didn't predict prices.
Official announcement
A filing the company must make with the US regulator (the SEC) when something important happens: results, a big deal, a change of boss. It's the company's own statement, so it's fact rather than opinion. Tap one to read the filing.
In short
A few sentences an AI wrote from that company's articles, for the companies you chose. It can miss things; the articles are underneath.
